In The Matrix Reloaded, when Neo encounters the Architect for the first time and asks, “Why I am here”, the Architect replies as follows: <br>
  <span style="color:green">“Your life is the sum of a remainder of an unbalanced equation inherent to the programming of the Matrix. You are the eventuality of an anomaly, which despite my sincerest efforts, I have been unable to eliminate from what is otherwise a harmony of mathematical precision. While it remains a burden assiduously avoided, it is not unexpected, and thus not beyond a measure of control. Which has led you, inexorably, here.” </span><br>
What this means is that there is no direct causation of Neo’s existence, why his life till this point (and also till the time he was presented the two pills by Morpheus) led him to Architect - in the most abstract sense. Had it not been Neo, it would have been someone else. But someone *would* have met the Architect in that room. The subject (in this case Neo) is not important, it’s the action, the event of an encounter between the Architect and the anomaly that is important.
<br><br>
Similarly, I sometimes believe that there is no direct reason for my foul or depressed mood - it’s the eventuality, the culmination of various small forces in play that makes my knees buckle and I find myself bereft of any hope, any zeal, any optimism.<br>
Whatever I feel during these minor events prove to be small plots of a bigger story - it’s all a build-up to something bigger; like I wouldn't have been able to ask these existential questions had I not gone through those events. <br><br>

I find myself on a very high-rise tower - the tower being constructed of jenga blocks, each block representing some thought, some belief. Then slowly and steadily, each jenga block is being removed to be subjected to questions and scrutiny. The most troubling moments are during the climax - when I find the ideas fundamental to my being questioned and invalidated. This leads to the lower blocks - blocks that laid the foundation of me as an individual - being completely removed. The tragedy is that I don’t fall down: there is no base, I am just floating in space with no gravity to provide me an anchor.<br>
I am searching for my anchor, but am unable to trust any of the n number of them available out there. I am afraid that the new one will abandon me just like the previous one did, that when I’ll look in the eyes of this new anchor, I’ll find nothing but disgust, pity for the previous one - the one which kept me hinged, which kept me grounded for almost my entire life. As a result, whenever I find a new place which seems to have a potential of being somewhere where I can rest and build myself from the bottom up, I attack it vehemently. I spit out all the venom I have for the previous one, for it left me stranded in the middle of nowhere. I barrage this new place to check if it’ll sustain my blows. Being in a very nascent stage, having no base whatsoever to hold against my punches, it is unable to take so much fire and gets annihilated.<br><br>

But maybe you know all of this. you are a specialist in this kind of situation, you must have dealt with so many of me(s) who are stuck in this Matrix looking desperately for a way out. You look at us through a glass wall - observe us banging our bodies across the insurmountable barriers, guiding us to find a weak spot with a hope that we’ll be able to find a crack from where we would be able to escape. The only reason I am writing all this is because this is a way for me to stop hurling myself across in a brownian motion, to let go of the maddening energy, choose a spot to sit and plan my escape from here with poise and control.
