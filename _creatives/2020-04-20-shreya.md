This is one of those entries where I would have loved to have a joint in my hand. I would have crushed the weed, licked the paper, and rolled a perfect joint. After that, I would have left the ashtray and the lighter next to my seat in order to go to the kitchen and fill my water bottle -  I know it is going to be a long night. I would have fetched two cushions from the living room, one for the back support, and another to keep on my lap so that the laptop’s heat doesn’t trouble me. <br> 
The next task would have been to find a piece of music long enough to get me through the length of this note, at the same time ambient enough to not disturb my train of thoughts. I would have chosen Ravi Shankar over his daughter this time, the calmness he brings while making me detached with the music is extraordinary. With everything in place, I would have opened a new note on Evernote, and only then I would have lighted one. In the couple of drags I would have taken, I would have thought about the outline of this note. Mentally I would have lived the day again, reminding myself why I am here, preparing myself to give words to my feelings. After creating the blueprint, I would have started the execution.<br><br>

But we don’t have one, and I have stuff to do after this. We’ll get everything in place except the joint and start nonetheless. However, the joint would have filled a vacuum which I feel right now. With the joint in hand, the feeling of loss would have been less profound. The same feeling of loss which has made me come here and write. On this particular night, I feel tired of the weight of loneliness I have been carrying. So much that I don’t feel like talking about it. I ask myself, what will I gain from writing about it, what’s the point of writing something that I felt some time back, what’s the point in feeling that again, what the point in making it immortal ?<br><br>

I do not curse the events which brought me here. Whatever happened, the reason why I am here was a welcome event. I had someone make me sit, get rid of all the distractions, and then ask: tell me something about yourself. There were some questions which were thrown at me. I replied in all honesty, not knowing when such a time will come again, when someone would like to hear my side of the story so earnestly. We talked for a while, we shared our insecurities, our fears, our thoughts. We didn’t try to reach a solution. Getting to an answer was never the goal. <br>
We were two kids telling each other how they tripped while playing, two friends telling each other their stories, two soldiers talking about the wounds they have survived, two travellers talking about the difficult days of their journey. After all the serious talks were over, we shared a laugh or two. A bit concerned about the lovely friend we made on this journey, we asked the other to be a bit careful on the path ahead as we started towards our paths. And that’s how we parted: wishing for happiness in other’s life, hoping they will sail through without much trouble. That’s how we said: Goodbye.
